Projectiles and Motion (WAEC Comprehensive Notes)

1. Fundamental Definition

A projectile is any object launched into flight through open space that continues moving forward by its own inertia and is influenced exclusively by gravity and air resistance. In the WAEC curriculum, air resistance is assumed to be negligible.

2. Resolving Initial Velocity Vectors

When an object is launched with an initial velocity \(u\) at an angle \(\theta\) to the horizontal plane, its movement must be broken down into two separate dimensions:

  • Horizontal Component (\(u_x\)): Remains uniform throughout the entire flight because no horizontal force acts on it. Formula: \(u_x = u \cos\theta\)
  • Vertical Component (\(u_y\)): Changes constantly due to the downward acceleration of gravity (\(g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2\)). Formula: \(u_y = u \sin\theta\)

3. Core Equations of Flight

You must memorize these three primary formulas for the WAEC structural calculation section:

Time of Flight (\(T\)): Total time the projectile spending airborne.
$$T = \frac{2u \sin\theta}{g}$$
Maximum Height (\(H\)): The highest vertical altitude attained.
$$H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$$
Horizontal Range (\(R\)): The total horizontal distance covered.
$$R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$$

Crucial Examination Insight: Horizontal range is maximized when the launch angle \(\theta = 45^\circ\), because \(\sin(2 \times 45^\circ) = \sin 90^\circ = 1\).
