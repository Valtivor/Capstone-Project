1. The Quadratic Formula

Overview: Used to find the roots (x-intercepts) of any quadratic equation in standard form.

Standard Form: \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) (where \(a \neq 0\))

To solve for \(x\), apply the quadratic formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

Quick Note: The value under the square root, \(b^2 - 4ac\), is called the discriminant. If it is negative, the equation has no real roots.

2. The Pythagorean Theorem

Overview: A fundamental principle in geometry that states the relationship between the sides of a right-angled triangle.

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$
Key Reminder: The variable \(c\) always represents the hypotenuse, which is the longest side and is always directly opposite the 90-degree angle.
